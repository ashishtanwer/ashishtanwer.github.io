What a consistency model is

A diffusion model samples by solving an ODE, the probability-flow ODE, from pure noise at σmax​ down to a clean image, in tens of network calls. Every point on one solution trajectory ends at the same image. A consistency model learns that end point directly: a function f(xσ​,σ) that returns the trajectory's start xϵ​ from any point xσ​ on it, so one call on pure noise gives a sample.

The page records OpenAI's class-conditional ImageNet 64 × 64 model, distilled from an EDM diffusion model with an L2 distance: 296 M parameters, 1,000 classes, noise levels from ϵ=0.002 to σmax​=80, sampled in one call or two.

The consistency function

Song et al. require f(x,ϵ)=x: at the smallest noise level the image is its own answer. Rather than hoping the network learns this, they build it into the parametrisation, with the network Fθ​ wrapped in noise-dependent scalings as in EDM:

fθ​(xσ​,σ)=cskip​(σ)xσ​+cout​(σ)Fθ​(cin​(σ)xσ​,σ,y)
cskip​=(σ−ϵ)2+σd2​σd2​​,cout​=σ2+σd2​​σd​(σ−ϵ)​,cin​=σ2+σd2​​1​

with σd​=0.5, the standard deviation of the images. At σ=ϵ, cskip​=1 and cout​=0, so the boundary condition holds for any weights; at σ=80 the output is almost all network. The Skip and out scalings panel draws both curves and marks the levels the page uses; the panel under it splits one f into its two terms, followed by a clip to the image range. cin​ keeps the U-Net's input at unit variance, and the U-Net reads the noise level as 250lnσ.

Consistency distillation

Training needs pairs of points on the same trajectory. Consistency distillation gets them from a pretrained diffusion model, the teacher ϕ. The noise range is cut into N levels ϵ=σ1​<⋯<σN​=σmax​ (Karras spacing, ρ=7; N=40 for this model). A training image is noised to σn+1​, the teacher takes one ODE solver step back to σn​, and the model is trained to give the same answer at both points:

LCD​=E[λ(σn​)d(fθ​(xσn+1​​,σn+1​),fθ−​(x^σn​ϕ​,σn​))]

θ− is an exponential moving average of θ, a slowly moving target that keeps training stable; d is a distance, here the squared L2 norm (the paper also uses LPIPS). Because f is pinned to the identity at ϵ, matching neighbours level by level propagates the right answer up to σmax​. The same paper also trains consistency models without a teacher (consistency training), replacing the solver step with an unbiased estimate from the clean image.

One call or two

One call maps xT​=80z to an image. Quality improves with a second call: add noise back to a middle level and apply f again.

x^0​=fθ​(xT​,T),xσ2​​=x^0​+σ22​−ϵ2​z′,x^0′​=fθ​(xσ2​​,σ2​)

The page uses the two-call schedule the authors' code uses for this model, timesteps [22, 0], so σ2​=1.382. The One call or two panel shows both images and where they differ: the second call keeps the layout and redraws the detail that noise at σ2​ covers. The Sampling strip below steps through both calls with their cin​, cskip​ and cout​.

Consistent only along trajectories

Self-consistency is a property of the probability-flow ODE's trajectories, not of every noisy version of an image. The Consistency along a noising path panel shows this. It takes the two-call image, adds one fixed noise at increasing scale, xσ​=x^0​+σz′ for σ = 0.002, 0.256, 1.38, 4.37, 16.78 and 80, and applies f at each level. While the noise is small, f returns x^0​; once the noise dominates, the straight path has left the trajectory and f draws a different image of the class. The RMS distance under each tile measures how far it has moved.

The class-conditional U-Net

Fθ​ is the class-conditional U-Net of Dhariwal and Nichol's ADM, as used by EDM on ImageNet 64: widths 192, 384, 576 and 768 at 64, 32, 16 and 8 pixels, self-attention at 32, 16 and 8 pixels, and ResNet blocks whose second normalisation is scaled and shifted by an embedding. The Class conditioning panel shows how the label y enters: its learned 768-wide vector is added to the noise level's embedding. From the same xT​, one call each, the four labels give a macaw, a golden retriever, a volcano and a monarch butterfly.

Where it came from

Consistency models (Song, Dhariwal, Chen and Sutskever, 2023) built on the probability-flow ODE of score-based models and on EDM's parametrisation and noise schedule. Song and Dhariwal's improved techniques later trained consistency models from data alone, with no teacher, no moving-average target and Pseudo-Huber losses in place of LPIPS. Latent consistency models applied consistency distillation to Stable Diffusion's latent space with guidance folded in (see the LCM tab), and adversarial diffusion distillation took a different route to one step (see the Turbo tab).

Papers

  1. Consistency Models Yang Song, Prafulla Dhariwal, Mark Chen, Ilya Sutskever · ICML 2023 Introduces consistency models, their skip/out parametrisation, consistency distillation and training, and multistep sampling; releases the checkpoint this tab records.
  2. Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models Tero Karras, Miika Aittala, Timo Aila, Samuli Laine · NeurIPS 2022 EDM: the teacher, its σ-parametrised preconditioning (c_skip, c_out, c_in) and the Karras noise levels the model is distilled on.
  3. Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations Yang Song et al. · ICLR 2021 The probability-flow ODE whose trajectories a consistency model maps to their end point.
  4. Diffusion Models Beat GANs on Image Synthesis Prafulla Dhariwal, Alex Nichol · NeurIPS 2021 ADM, the class-conditional U-Net with scale-shift normalisation that the model uses as F_θ.
  5. Improved Techniques for Training Consistency Models Yang Song, Prafulla Dhariwal · ICLR 2024 Consistency training from data alone: no EMA target, Pseudo-Huber losses in place of LPIPS, a lognormal noise schedule.
  6. Latent Consistency Models: Synthesizing High-Resolution Images with Few-Step Inference Simian Luo, Yiqin Tan, Longbo Huang, Jian Li, Hang Zhao · arXiv 2023 Consistency distillation in Stable Diffusion's latent space, with classifier-free guidance built in; the LCM tab.
