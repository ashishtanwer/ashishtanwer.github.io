What Stable Audio Open is

Stable Audio Open generates stereo sound at 44.1 kHz from a text prompt and a requested length. It is a latent diffusion model with two departures from AudioLDM (see the AudioLDM tab): its autoencoder compresses the waveform itself rather than a spectrogram, and its denoiser is a transformer over the latent's frames, conditioned on the prompt and on the clip's timing.

The page records the released 1.0 model on The sound of a hammer hitting a wooden surface., 10 s long, against the negative prompt Low quality., in 20 sampler steps with guidance 7 and seed 0. The transformer has 1.06 B parameters in 24 blocks; the T5-base text encoder and the VAE add 110 M and 156 M. The board shows block 1 in full at three steps.

A waveform autoencoder

The Autoencoder card shows the Oobleck VAE: its encoder turns every 2,048 stereo samples into one latent frame of 64 channels, about 21.5 frames a second, and its decoder inverts that. The decoder upsamples with five transposed convolutions by 8, 8, 4, 4 and 2, each followed by residual units with the periodic Snake activation,

Snake(x)=x+β1​sin2(αx)

which suits signals made of oscillations. The 10-second clip is 216 latent frames, 64 × 216 = 13,824 numbers for 882,000 samples. The transformer always denoises 1,024 frames (47.5 s), the longest clip the model makes; the pipeline cuts the decoded audio to the 10 s asked for.

Conditioning without adaLN

Image DiTs feed the timestep into every block through adaptive LayerNorm. Stable Audio Open's blocks have none; everything enters as tokens. The Conditioning card shows the pieces. Two timing numbers, where the clip starts in its source recording (0 s) and how long the source is (10 s), each pass through learned Fourier features and a Linear:

s′=512clamp(s,0,512)​,e(s)=W[s′, sin(2πs′w), cos(2πs′w)]+b
  • The cross-attention context is the prompt's 128 T5 vectors followed by the two timing embeddings, passed through a small MLP: 130 rows of 768.
  • A global token, the two timing embeddings plus the embedding of the noise level t=π2​arctanσ, is prepended to the 1,024 latent frames as token 0, so every frame can read the timestep through self-attention.

Training clips are cut from longer recordings, and the timing numbers say which part each one is. That is how the model learns to end a sound on time inside the 47.5 s it always generates: the timing conditioning introduced by the first Stable Audio paper.

One block

Each of the 24 blocks is three pre-norm residual steps on 1,025 tokens of width 1,536:

x1​=x+SelfAttn(LN1​x),x2​=x1​+CrossAttn(LN2​x1​,c),x3​=x2​+SwiGLU(LN3​x2​)

The Self-Attention card has 24 heads of 64 over all 1,025 tokens. Positions enter as rotary embeddings on only the first 32 coordinates of each head; the other 32 carry no position, so part of every score depends on content alone. The frame map beside the board shows the selected query's weight over the clip's frames.

The Cross-Attention card reads the 130 context rows with 24 query heads sharing 12 key/value heads. The context has no mask: the 113 padding slots after the prompt are zero vectors, so each has key 0 and score 0 and they share one column of weight. The feed-forward is SwiGLU, W2​(Wv​u⊙SiLU(Wg​u)), from 1,536 to two halves of 6,144 and back.

v-prediction on EDM noise levels

The model is trained with the v-objective in EDM's parametrisation with data standard deviation 1: the latent is x=x0​+σϵ, the network reads x/σ2+1​ and its output v^ gives the clean estimate

x^0​=σ2+1x​−σ2+1​σ​v^,v^=vneg​+7(vprompt​−vneg​)

with classifier-free guidance against the negative prompt. The sampler is an SDE variant of DPM-Solver++ over 20 levels from σ=500 to 0.3. Each step shrinks the current latent, moves it towards the clean estimate D and adds fresh noise so that the noise level comes out at the next σs​:

xs​=σσs​​e−hx+(1−e−2h)D+σs​1−e−2h​z,h=logσs​σ​

Between the first and the last step D is a second-order estimate: x^0​ plus half a finite difference with the previous one. The noise z comes from a Brownian tree seeded by the generator, so the run repeats. The Sampling loop card shows the guided v^ over the clip's frames at every step. The paper's own samples use 100 DPM-Solver++ steps with guidance 7; this run uses the pipeline's 20.

Where it came from

Stable Audio (2024) introduced timing-conditioned latent audio diffusion with a U-Net; Stable Audio 2 replaced the U-Net with this transformer to generate long-form music. Stable Audio Open releases that design with open weights, trained only on Creative Commons recordings: 486,492 of them, 7,300 hours from Freesound and the Free Music Archive, according to its paper. Its denoiser shares the DiT family with the image and video tabs, but conditions through tokens rather than adaLN.
