What LTX-Video is

LTX-Video is a video diffusion transformer designed for speed. Its idea is to make the latent much smaller than other video models do: the VAE compresses each 32 × 32 × 8 block of pixels into one 128-channel vector, and that vector is one transformer token, with no patchify in the transformer. Fewer tokens make full 3-D attention cheap; the detail the latent cannot hold is restored by a VAE decoder that takes the last denoising step itself.

The page records LTX-Video 2B v0.9.1: a 1,923 M-parameter transformer of 28 blocks and a 935 M VAE, generating 17 frames of 384 × 256 (25 frames a second) for A corgi running on a sunny beach, waves in the background in 50 flow-matching steps, guidance 3 and seed 2. The board shows block 1 in full at steps 11 and 36.

A 1:192 video VAE

The VAE's first operation is the patchify: each 4 × 4 square of pixels is laid side by side into 48 channels. Its convolutions then compress 8 × in time and 8 × more in space, so one latent position covers 8 frames × 32 × 32 pixels × 3 colours, 24,576 numbers, in 128 channels: the ratio of 192 in the Video VAE · 1:192 card's title. Like the other video VAEs it is causal in time and gives frame 0 its own latent frame, so this 17-frame clip becomes 3 × 8 × 12 × 128 = 36,864 numbers, 136 times fewer than its 5,013,504 pixel values.

That makes the sequence short: 3 × 8 × 12 = 288 tokens, against 1,920 for Wan's 17 frames of the same size (see the Wan tab). Self-attention costs grow with the square of the sequence length, which is where most of LTX-Video's speed comes from.

The transformer block

The LTX-Video paper builds its transformer on PixArt-α's DiT, with RMSNorm, normalised queries and keys and rotary positions added. Each block is conditioned on the timestep through adaLN (a shift, scale and gate from t around self-attention and the feed-forward), then reads the prompt through cross-attention:

x←x+α1​⋅SelfAttn((1+γ1​)rms(x)+β1​),x←x+CrossAttn(x,c),x←x+α2​⋅FFN((1+γ2​)rms(x)+β2​)

In the 3-D Self-Attention card every one of the 288 tokens attends to all the others across the three latent frames. Queries and keys are RMS-normalised across all 32 heads of 64, then rotated:

wij​=softmaxj​(64​Ri​rms(qi​)⋅Rj​rms(kj​)​)

The Cross-Attention card reads the prompt from T5-XXL, padded to 128 tokens; unlike Wan and CogVideoX, the padding is masked out of the softmax, so only the 16 real tokens get weight. The LTX-Video paper reports that this cross-attention worked better for it than MMDiT-style joint attention.

Rotary positions in seconds and pixels

Positions enter as rotary embeddings (RoPE): each query and key is turned pair by pair by angles proportional to its position, so attention scores depend on the offset between tokens. LTX-Video measures position in physical units rather than token indices. A token's frame, row and column are scaled to seconds and pixels (one latent frame is 8 frames at 25 frames a second, 0.32 s; one row or column is 32 pixels), divided by fixed maxima so they fall in [−1, 1], and each turns 341 pairs at frequencies spaced exponentially from 1 to 104⋅π/2.

Unlike Wan, which rotates each head separately, the rotation covers all 2,048 channels before they split into heads. The paper found such fractional, normalised coordinates worked best; they also keep positions comparable across resolutions and frame rates. The 3-D RoPE card shows the selected token's frame, row and column parts.

Flow matching with a stretched shift

Training follows rectified flow: xσ​=(1−σ)x0​+σϵ and the model predicts the velocity v=ϵ−x0​. Sampling takes Euler steps from σ=1, with classifier-free guidance against the negative prompt worst quality, inconsistent motion, blurry, jittery, distorted:

v^=v∅​+3(vc​−v∅​),xσ′​=xσ​+(σ′−σ)v^,x^0​=xσ​−σv^

The 50 levels are shifted towards high noise by σ↦eμσ/(eμσ+1−σ), with μ growing with the number of tokens, the resolution-dependent shift of Stable Diffusion 3; then the schedule is stretched so its last level before 0 is σ=0.1. Step 11 runs at σ=0.895 and step 36 at σ=0.494. The Flow matching card plays the loop and lists the selected token's numbers at each step.

The decoder's last step

A latent compressed 1:192 cannot hold every fine detail. LTX-Video's decoder is therefore itself a denoiser: it is conditioned on a timestep through adaptive normalisation, trained at small noise levels, and injects noise inside some of its blocks, so it produces the clean video from a slightly noisy latent instead of only upsampling. The pipeline mixes a little noise back into the transformer's final latent and decodes at a small timestep:

z′=(1−0.025)z+0.025ϵ,video=D(z′,t=0.03)

The Decoder's last step card shows z, ϵ and z′ for the selected token and the frames decoded this way next to a plain decode of z at t=0. The decoder's blocks are shifted and scaled by t as it upsamples 32 × in space and 8 × in time.

Where it came from

LTX-Video (Lightricks, 2024) combines a DiT backbone in the PixArt-α line with rectified flow and a VAE moved to the centre of the design: compression that would lose too much detail elsewhere is made safe by a decoder that denoises. The other video tabs keep a 4 × 8 × 8 VAE with a 2 × 2 patchify: CogVideoX with joint text–video attention and Wan with cross-attention.

Papers

  1. LTX-Video: Realtime Video Latent Diffusion Yoav HaCohen et al. · arXiv 2024 The model this tab records: the 1:192 VAE that absorbs the patchify, the denoising decoder, fractional RoPE and rectified-flow training.
  2. PixArt-α: Fast Training of Diffusion Transformer for Photorealistic Text-to-Image Synthesis Junsong Chen et al. · ICLR 2024 The text-to-image DiT with T5 cross-attention that LTX-Video's transformer builds on (see the PixArt-α tab).
  3. Flow Matching for Generative Modeling Yaron Lipman et al. · ICLR 2023 Training a velocity field along a chosen path from noise to data, the objective LTX-Video samples with Euler steps.
  4. Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis Patrick Esser et al. · ICML 2024 Rectified-flow transformers and the resolution-dependent timestep shift that LTX-Video's sampler applies with μ from the token count.
  5. RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding Jianlin Su et al. · arXiv 2021 Rotary position embedding, which LTX-Video applies to normalised frame, row and column coordinates.
  6. Scalable Diffusion Models with Transformers William Peebles, Saining Xie · ICCV 2023 DiT: a transformer over latent tokens conditioned through adaLN, the backbone family of every video tab.
  7. Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer Colin Raffel et al. · JMLR 2020 T5, whose XXL encoder (v1.1) gives the prompt vectors LTX-Video cross-attends to.
