What retention is

Retention (Sun et al., 2023) is attention without the softmax, with a fixed decay in its place. Dropping the softmax lets the layer be rewritten as a recurrence over a fixed-size state, and the decay keeps that state from growing without bound. The RetNet paper gives three equivalent ways to compute it: in parallel over the whole sequence for training, recurrently token by token for decoding at O(1) cost per token, and chunk by chunk for long sequences.

This tab is a formula demo on GPT-2 small's recorded queries, keys and values at layers 1, 3, 6, 9 and 12. GPT-2 was trained with softmax attention, so the page shows what retention's formula does with real vectors, not what a trained RetNet computes. Every constant comes from the paper and its torchscale code; nothing is learned for the demo.

From recurrence to matrix

Start from a state Sn​, a d×d matrix that decays by γ and absorbs each new key-value pair, and read it with the query:

Sn​=γSn−1​+kn⊤​vn​,on​=qn​Sn​=m≤n∑​γn−m(qn​⋅km​)vm​

The right-hand side is a weighted sum of values, so for the whole sequence at once

Retention(X)=(QK⊤⊙D)V,Dnm​=γn−m[m≤n]

The Parallel retention card draws QK⊤⊙D for one head (the QKᵀ ⊙ D view), with the decay alone (Decay D) and the raw products (QKᵀ) as the other two views. The weights are signed and need not sum to one; RetNet normalises each head's output afterwards instead.

Positions: the Θ rotation

Before the product, queries and keys are rotated by angles proportional to their positions, qn​→Θn​qn​ and km​→Θm​km​, turning each pair of dimensions (2i,2i+1) by nθi​. The product then depends only on the offset n−m, as with rotary embeddings (RoFormer). RetNet takes the rotation from xPos (Sun et al., 2022). The demo uses torchscale's angles θi​=10000−i/31 for i=0,…,31 and divides keys by 64​; the Θ boxes on the card open the rotation.

Multi-scale decay

Each head gets its own decay, fixed by the paper rather than learned:

γh​=1−2−5−h,h=0,…,11

Head 1 has γ=0.96875, so its weights halve about every 22 tokens; head 12 has γ=0.99998474 and halves only after about 45,000. The toolbar gives each head's half-life. Short-memory heads concentrate on recent tokens, long-memory heads keep nearly undecayed sums over the whole prompt, and between them the heads cover many time scales.

The decay is a cousin of ALiBi (the ALiBi tab). ALiBi subtracts a penalty proportional to distance from each score before the softmax, which multiplies the exponentiated scores by a geometric factor e−c(n−m); retention multiplies the raw products by γn−m and does not normalise.

The recurrent form

The Recurrent form panel runs the same computation token by token. It draws the head's 64 × 64 state after the selected token, the size of the state at every token (it shrinks by γ each step and grows by km⊤​vm​), and the selected token's output from both forms. The state has the same size however long the prompt is, which is the point: a decoding step reads one d×d matrix instead of a growing key-value cache. Over every token, head and recorded layer, the two float32 results differ by at most 1.2×10−4, or 2.9×10−7 of the largest output.

For long sequences RetNet uses the chunkwise form between the two: the parallel product inside each chunk, and a state passed from chunk to chunk as in the recurrence.

The full layer and where it came from

A RetNet layer does more than this page shows: each head's output is normalised with GroupNorm, multiplied by a swish gate swish(XWG​) and projected by WO​. GPT-2 has no gate weights WG​, so the demo stops at the heads.

Retention belongs to the linear-attention family. Katharopoulos et al. (2020) showed that attention without the softmax is a recurrent network, and Schlag et al. (2021) read its state as a fast-weight memory written by outer products. RetNet added the fixed per-head decay and the rotation. Later layers made the decay depend on the input: Mamba-2, whose scalar-decay form contains retention as the case of a constant step (the Mamba-2 tab), and RWKV-7 (the Linear tab).

Papers

  1. Retentive Network: A Successor to Transformer for Large Language Models Yutao Sun et al. · arXiv 2023 Retention: parallel, recurrent and chunkwise forms, the multi-scale decay and the full RetNet layer.
  2. A Length-Extrapolatable Transformer Yutao Sun et al. · ACL 2023 xPos, the relative-position rotation that RetNet's Θ follows.
  3. Transformers are RNNs: Fast Autoregressive Transformers with Linear Attention Angelos Katharopoulos et al. · ICML 2020 Linear attention and its recurrent form, retention without the decay.
  4. Linear Transformers Are Secretly Fast Weight Programmers Imanol Schlag, Kazuki Irie, Jürgen Schmidhuber · ICML 2021 Reads the linear-attention state as a fast-weight memory written by outer products, the state drawn on the page.
  5. RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding Jianlin Su et al. · arXiv 2021 Rotary position embeddings, the rotation that xPos and RetNet build on.
  6. Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners Alec Radford et al. · 2019 GPT-2, whose recorded queries, keys and values this demo runs through retention.
