π0's design, made small

SmolVLA (Shukor et al., Hugging Face) follows π0's recipe, a vision-language model that reads the scene and an action expert that generates a chunk of actions by flow matching, but cuts it down so it can be trained on one GPU and run on consumer hardware. Its authors report performance comparable to VLAs ten times its size. Three choices do most of the saving: a small VLM, fewer visual tokens, and only the first half of the VLM's layers.

This page runs LeRobot's SmolVLA fine-tuned on LIBERO, in bfloat16 on a Mac, on the same LIBERO-Spatial task as the π0 tab: “pick up the black bowl between the plate and the ramekin and place it on the plate”. It has 403 M parameters in use (the checkpoint's language-model head is never run) and finishes the task in 79 steps at 20 Hz with two 50-action chunks.

Fewer visual tokens

The VLM is SmolVLM2-500M. Each camera frame, resized to 512 × 512, is cut into 16-pixel patches, 32×32=1,024 patches of 768 numbers from its SigLIP encoder. A pixel shuffle then packs every 4 × 4 block of neighbouring patches into one vector, laying the 16 patches end to end (16×768=12,288 numbers), and a linear layer maps it to the VLM's width of 960:

1024 patches×768 4×4→1 ​64×12288 linear ​64 tokens×960

So each camera costs 64 tokens instead of 1,024 (the Visual token reduction panel; clicking a token shows which patches it packs). With two cameras, the instruction padded to 48 tokens and one state token, What the VLM reads is a prefix of 177 tokens, against π0's 816 image and text tokens.

Half a VLM and an interleaved expert

SmolVLA keeps only the first 16 of SmolVLM2's 32 layers: its authors found the middle layers' features good enough for the action head, and skipping the rest halves the VLM's cost. Attention uses grouped queries, 15 query heads sharing 5 key/value heads of 64 dimensions. The prefix runs once per chunk through the 16 kept layers, and each layer caches its keys and values.

The action expert has one layer per kept VLM layer and is 720 wide. Its layers alternate (the Backbone and action expert panel). In a self-attention layer the actions read that layer's cached prefix plus the actions before them. In a cross-attention layer the expert's own key and value maps read the VLM layer's cached keys and values, and the actions do not see each other. The expert runs 10 times per chunk, once per Euler step, against the same cache.

The mask and the flow

The Attention mask panel builds the mask from one flag per token, 0 for images and words and 1 for the state and every action. The running sum of the flags numbers the blocks, and a token may read another whose number is not larger:

So images and words see each other but not the state, and the actions are causal: action aj​ reads a1​…aj​, which the Actions reading actions map shows as a lower triangle. In π0 the actions see each other in both directions.

The flow is π0's. From noise at t=1 to actions at t=0, the expert predicts a velocity and 10 Euler steps of x←x−0.1vθ​(xt​,t,o) carry the chunk from noise to 50 actions of 7 numbers; training regresses vθ​ onto ϵ−A along the straight path xt​=tϵ+(1−t)A. Asynchronous inference

Run synchronously, the robot stops while the model predicts the next chunk: in this episode it waits 15 control ticks for the first chunk and 9 for the second. SmolVLA's asynchronous inference separates prediction from execution. The robot keeps playing its queue of actions and asks for a new chunk as soon as the queue falls below a threshold, here half a chunk; when the new chunk arrives, the actions it shares with the old queue are blended by a weighted average.

The Asynchronous inference panel replays LeRobot's rule in simulated time from the same start, each chunk arriving after its measured inference time. 3 of the 5 runs, with different noise seeds, finished the task; the one shown played 79 actions in 88 ticks, idle only while the first chunk was computed. The other two had not finished after 280 steps: different sampling noise and timing can change the outcome. Real-time chunking (Black et al., 2025) addresses the seams between chunks more carefully, by freezing the actions that will execute and inpainting the rest.

Where it came from

SmolVLA (2025) builds on SmolVLM, a family of small VLMs designed around aggressive token reduction, and on π0's flow-matching action expert. It was pretrained on community-collected datasets from low-cost robots rather than on large industrial collections, and released with its training code and data. GR00T (next tab) pairs a frozen VLM with a separate diffusion transformer that cross-attends to the VLM's output.
