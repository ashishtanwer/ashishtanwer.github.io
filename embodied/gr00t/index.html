Two systems

GR00T N1 (NVIDIA, 2025) is a vision-language-action model for humanoid robots built as two coupled parts, named after the fast and slow modes of thinking. System 2 is a vision-language model that reads the camera and the instruction; System 1 is a diffusion transformer (DiT) that turns noise into a chunk of motor actions by flow matching, reading System 2's output at every other block. GR00T N1.5, the model on this page, keeps that design, freezes the VLM in both pretraining and fine-tuning, and starts the VLM from Eagle 2.5 tuned for grounding.

The page records GR00T N1.5 3B, 2.72 B parameters: a 1.34 B VLM, frozen, and a 550 M DiT, plus a trained adapter and per-robot encoders. It runs open loop on episode 0 of Isaac-GR00T's simulated pick-and-place demonstrations for the Fourier GR-1 humanoid's arms and hands, “pick the pear from the counter and place it in the plate”: every 16th recorded observation goes in, a 16-step chunk comes out, and the page compares it with the 16 recorded actions.

System 2: a frozen VLM, cut at layer 12

The System 2 · Eagle 2.5 VLM view follows the camera and instruction through the VLM. The 224 × 224 ego camera frame goes through a SigLIP encoder (27 layers, 1,152 wide) as 16 × 16 patches of 14 pixels, and a small connector MLP turns the 256 patches into tokens for the language model, which reads them inside a chat template together with the instruction.

The language model is Qwen3-1.7B, but GR00T keeps only its first 12 of 28 layers, and the checkpoint has no weights for the rest. Its attention is grouped-query, 16 query heads sharing 8 key/value heads of 128 dimensions, with RMS-normalised queries and keys and rotary positions; the Layer 12 attention card shows the instruction words reading each other and the image, causally. The Features are layer 12's output after the final norm, 2,048 numbers per token. The VL adapter, a LayerNorm and four self-attention blocks trained with the action head, then turns every token into the keys and values the DiT's cross-attention reads. Freezing the VLM keeps its language understanding intact while the adapter learns what the actions need.

System 1: a DiT over state and actions

The System 1 · DiT action head view opens blocks 1 and 2 of 16. The DiT's input is 49 tokens of width 1,536: one for the robot's state, 32 learned future tokens (not drawn), and the 16 actions of the noisy chunk. Odd blocks cross-attend from these tokens to all the VLM's tokens; even blocks self-attend among the 49 tokens, with no mask. Both use 32 heads of 48 dimensions:

wij​=softmaxj​(48​qi​⋅kj​​),oi​=j∑​wij​vj​,

with keys and values from the VL adapter in a cross-attention block and from the 49 tokens in a self-attention block. The flow time enters every block through adaptive LayerNorm, as in Peebles and Xie's DiT: the timestep t becomes a sinusoid of ⌊1000t⌋ at 128 frequencies, an MLP turns it into an embedding, and each block computes its own scale and shift from it,

adaLN(x,t)=LN(x)⊙(1+γb​(t))+βb​(t).

The Timestep · adaLN card shows the sinusoid, the embedding and the size of every block's γ and β.

Four steps of flow matching

GR00T integrates the flow from noise at t=0 to actions at t=1, the opposite direction to the π0 and SmolVLA tabs. Training interpolates between Gaussian noise ϵ and the normalised chunk A and regresses the velocity along the straight line:

xt​=(1−t)ϵ+tA,L=Et,ϵ​​vθ​(xt​,t,o)−(A−ϵ)​2.

At run time four Euler steps, at t=0, ¼, ½ and ¾, each move the chunk by a quarter of the predicted velocity, x←x+41​vθ​(xt​,t,o). The Flow matching strip shows x and v at each step as 32 rows (the GR-1 arms' 26 joints, then 6 padding) by 16 steps; at the end the chunk is unnormalised from the dataset's per-joint minimum and maximum to radians.

One model, many bodies

Robots differ in how many joints they have, so GR00T pads states to 64 numbers and actions to 32 and gives each embodiment its own small encoders and decoder. The Per-body encoders card draws the 32-slot weight banks of the state encoder, the action encoder and the action decoder; the checkpoint trained the slots of four embodiments, and GR-1 uses slot 24. The state's 26 joint angles go in as their sines and cosines, padded to 64, and become one token; the action encoder combines each noisy action with the flow time and adds a learned position; the decoder maps each of the last 16 DiT outputs, 1,024 numbers, back to 32.

The Episode · open loop card plots one joint's recorded actions against every predicted chunk. Over the run the arms' mean squared error is 0.0425 rad². The hands are left out, as Isaac-GR00T's evaluation script does by default: they get binary open and close commands, the model switches the right hand at other steps than the recording, and the idle left hand is recorded at −1.5 while the model holds it at 0.

Where it came from

GR00T N1 (2025) combined a VLM with a flow-matching DiT, trained on real robot data, human videos and synthetic trajectories, and deployed on the GR-1 humanoid. Its action head follows π0's flow matching and Peebles and Xie's diffusion transformer with adaptive LayerNorm; its System 2 is built from NVIDIA's Eagle VLMs, which here combine SigLIP with Qwen3. N1.5 froze the VLM, simplified the connector and added an objective that aligns the model with embeddings of future observations.
