What Oasis is

Oasis is a world model of Minecraft from Decart and Etched. Given the frames so far and the player's actions, it generates the next frame, one frame at a time, so a player can walk, look around and break blocks in a world that exists only as the model's predictions. Its authors describe two transformers: a ViT autoencoder that compresses each frame, and a DiT that denoises the next frame's latent, trained with diffusion forcing.

The page records the released Oasis 500M checkpoint running open-oasis's own sampling loop: 32 frames at 360 × 640 from one prompt frame and a recorded stream of player actions, each new frame made in 10 denoising steps.

Diffusion forcing

In ordinary video diffusion all the frames of a clip share one noise level. Diffusion forcing gives every frame its own level kt​, drawn independently in training:

xtkt​​=αˉkt​​​xt​+1−αˉkt​​​ϵt​,kt​∈{0,…,999} drawn for each frame t

One network thus learns to denoise any mix: clean frames next to noisy ones, a nearly finished frame next to pure noise. That freedom is what allows frame-by-frame generation. At sampling time the newest frame starts as pure noise, clipped at ±20, and is denoised from level 999 to 0 in 10 steps, while the frames before it are already made. The sampler tells the model those frames sit at level 14 although it leaves them clean. The Diffusion forcing panel shows the level of every frame in the window at each step, and the newest frame's estimate x^0​ after each step.

Per-frame conditioning

Every frame carries its own conditioning vector, made from its noise level and its action:

ct​=MLP(sinusoid(kt​))+Wat​

The action at​ has 25 entries: keys such as forward, back, left, right, jump, sneak, sprint, attack and use, the nine hotbar slots and a few others, and the camera's horizontal and vertical movement; action t steers frame t. As in DiT, each block turns ct​ into a shift, a scale and a gate (adaLN) for the tokens of its own frame, separately for its spatial and temporal halves, so one forward pass can treat frames at different noise levels and with different actions differently. The Per-frame panel shows the actions over the window and c for the selected frame at each step.

Spatial and temporal attention

The DiT has 16 blocks of width 1,024 with 16 heads. A frame's latent, 18 × 32 × 16 values, is cut into 2 × 2 patches: 9 × 16 = 144 tokens. Each block splits attention into two halves, like the divided space–time attention of TimeSformer:

  • Spatial attention: each token attends to the 144 tokens of its own frame, with RoPE on row and column.
  • Temporal attention: each token attends to the tokens at the same position in its own and earlier frames of the window, at most 32 frames, with RoPE on time. It is causal: a frame never reads a later one.

Attending over 144 tokens and then over at most 32 frames costs far less than attending over all 144 × 32 tokens at once. The Spatial attention and Temporal attention panels show both for a token on the sky, a birch trunk, a plank block or the hotbar.

Denoising: v-prediction and DDIM

For every frame the network predicts v=αˉ​ϵ−1−αˉ​x0​, a target that keeps the same scale at every noise level, as Salimans and Ho proposed. The clean frame and the noise follow from it,

x^0​=αˉ​x−1−αˉ​v,ϵ^=1−αˉ​x+αˉ​v,

and a deterministic DDIM step moves only the newest frame to the next level, x′=αˉ′​x^0​+1−αˉ′​ϵ^. The schedule αˉ falls from 1 to 0 along a sigmoid over the 1,000 levels. The levels used are 999, 899, … 99, and the last step goes straight to the clean frame.

The ViT autoencoder and the rollout

The ViT autoencoder's encoder cuts a 360 × 640 frame into 20 × 20 patches and turns them into an 18 × 32 grid of 16-channel latents, scaled by 0.0784 before the DiT; its decoder, 12 transformer blocks, turns latents back into pixels. The Rollout strip shows all 32 frames: frame 0 is the given image, and every later one is made from the window of frames before it. Errors compound in such a loop, as the Oasis authors note: a small flaw in one frame becomes part of the context for all the frames after it.

Where it came from

DIAMOND and Google's GameNGen (both 2024) ran games inside diffusion models built on U-Nets. Diffusion Forcing (2024) gave autoregressive video diffusion a training scheme with an independent noise level per frame. Oasis (2024) combined it with a DiT and a ViT autoencoder, trained on Minecraft play with actions; the action stream on this page is in the format of OpenAI's VPT recordings. Matrix-Game 2.0, the previous tab, takes the same direction further with a pretrained video model, a key–value cache and few-step distillation.
