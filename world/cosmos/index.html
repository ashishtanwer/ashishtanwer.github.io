What Cosmos-Predict2 Video2World is

Cosmos-Predict2 is the diffusion branch of NVIDIA's Cosmos world foundation models. Its Video2World models take a first frame and a text prompt and generate the video that follows, all frames at once: a diffusion transformer denoises the latents of the whole clip together, with the given frame held clean. Unlike the action-conditioned tabs before it, it is steered by a description, not by a player, and it does not roll forward chunk by chunk.

The page records one call of the 2-billion-parameter model through diffusers, with NVIDIA's guardrail: a photo of a yellow scrubber on a dirty plate and a prompt describing the scrubbing, 13 frames at 352 × 640 and 16 frames per second, 35 denoising steps with guidance 7, seed 1.

Latents and the given frame

Like Matrix-Game, Cosmos-Predict2 uses the Wan 2.1 VAE: each latent pixel covers 8 × 8 pixels and 4 frames, so the 13 frames become 4 latent frames of 44 × 80 × 16 values, and 1 × 2 × 2 patches make 4 × 22 × 40 = 3,520 tokens. The given image is latent frame 0. A condition mask marks it, it stays at its clean latent through every step (the network is told its noise level is σ=10−4), and only latent frames 1 to 3 are denoised. The Video2World panel shows the clip's latent frames at the selected step.

The block

The diffusion transformer has 28 blocks of width 2,048 with 16 heads of 128. Each block has three parts, each modulated by the frame's own noise level through adaLN-LoRA: the shift, scale and gate come from a low-rank projection (rank 256), which saves parameters over DiT's full adaLN.

  • Self-attention over all 3,520 tokens of all frames, with 3-D RoPE over time, row and column.
  • Text cross-attention to the prompt, encoded by the T5-11B encoder into 512 positions, of which 143 are real tokens and the rest zero padding.
  • An MLP of width 8,192.

The Self-attention panel sums one token's weights per frame in blocks 1, 14 and 28, and the Text cross-attention panel shows which prompt tokens it reads.

EDM noise levels, flow-trained network

The sampler counts noise in EDM's terms, x=x0​+σϵ, with 35 levels spaced as in Karras et al. from σ=80 down to 0.002. The network was trained as a rectified flow, on the straight path xt​=(1−t)x0​+tϵ with velocity v=ϵ−x0​. The two meet through t=σ/(σ+1): scaling the EDM sample by 1−t gives exactly the flow sample,

(1−t)x=1+σx0​+σϵ​=(1−t)x0​+tϵ,x^0​=(1−t)x−tvθ​

Each step then takes an Euler step in σ towards the predicted clean latent, x←x+(σi+1​−σi​)(x−x^0​)/σi​, as DIAMOND does. The Sampling panel plots the 35 levels and decodes x^0​ at the recorded steps.

Classifier-free guidance

Each step runs the network twice, once with the prompt and once with a negative prompt that describes what to avoid (static, blurry, low-quality video), and pushes the estimate away from the second:

x^0guided​=x^0​+w(x^0​−x^0neg​),w=7

This is classifier-free guidance (Ho and Salimans). In their method the second prediction has no conditioning at all, and the difference between the two points towards what the prompt adds. Here the second prediction is conditioned on the negative prompt instead, so the guided estimate also moves away from what that prompt describes. A larger w follows the prompt more closely, at some cost in variety. NVIDIA's guardrail checks the prompts before generation and blurs faces in the decoded video after it.

Where it came from

The Cosmos platform (2025) released world models of both kinds, diffusion and autoregressive; the autoregressive Cosmos-Predict1 is the Cosmos AR tab. Cosmos-Predict2 pairs a diffusion transformer with the Wan 2.1 VAE and a network trained as a rectified flow, sampled on EDM's noise levels. It shares its machinery with text-to-video models, such as the Diffusion Explainer's Wan tab, and with Matrix-Game: what makes a video model a world model here is what it is conditioned on and how it is used, more than the network itself.
